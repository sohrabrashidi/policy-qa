# Customer Due Diligence Policy

> Sample document written for this demo. "Harbor Point Exchange" is a fictional company and the figures below are illustrative, not regulatory guidance.

## 1. Purpose and scope

This policy sets out how Harbor Point Exchange identifies and verifies customers before carrying out currency exchange or money transfer transactions. It applies to all branches, the mobile app and any agent acting on the company's behalf.

## 2. When due diligence is required

Customer due diligence (CDD) must be completed before:

- opening a customer profile for money transfers, regardless of amount;
- any single currency exchange transaction of 3,000 KWD or more, or its equivalent in another currency;
- linked transactions by the same customer on the same day that together reach 3,000 KWD;
- any transaction where staff suspect money laundering or terrorist financing, regardless of amount.

## 3. Standard due diligence

### 3.1 Individuals

Staff must record the customer's full name as shown on the identity document, date of birth, nationality, residential address, phone number and occupation. The original Civil ID or a valid passport must be checked in person, or verified electronically through the approved identity provider for app customers.

A copy of the identity document must be stored in the customer file. Expired identity documents are not acceptable.

### 3.2 Companies

For corporate customers, staff must obtain the commercial licence, the authorised signatory list, and the identity documents of every beneficial owner holding 25% or more of the company. Transactions must be signed by an authorised signatory.

## 4. Enhanced due diligence

### 4.1 When it applies

Enhanced due diligence (EDD) applies to:

- politically exposed persons (PEPs), their family members and close associates;
- customers sending to or receiving from high-risk jurisdictions listed in the company risk register;
- customers whose transfers in a rolling 30-day period exceed 10,000 KWD;
- any customer rated "high risk" by the transaction monitoring system.

### 4.2 What EDD involves

EDD requires documented source of funds (for example a salary certificate, bank statement or sale agreement) and approval from the branch manager before the transaction is processed. For PEPs, approval from the Compliance Officer is also required.

EDD customers are reviewed at least every 12 months, or sooner if their risk rating changes.

## 5. Ongoing monitoring

Customer information must be refreshed every 3 years for low-risk customers and every 12 months for high-risk customers. Staff must update the profile whenever the customer reports a change of address, nationality, or occupation.

## 6. Refusing or stopping a relationship

If a customer refuses to provide required information, the transaction must not be processed. Staff must not tell the customer that a suspicious activity report is being considered. The case is escalated to the Compliance Officer the same working day.
