# Transaction Monitoring Procedure

> Sample document written for this demo. "Harbor Point Exchange" is a fictional company and the figures below are illustrative, not regulatory guidance.

## 1. Overview

All money transfers and exchange transactions are screened in real time and reviewed again in a daily batch. The aim is to detect unusual patterns early, without blocking legitimate customers for longer than needed.

## 2. Real-time screening

### 2.1 Sanctions screening

Every sender and beneficiary name is screened against the consolidated sanctions lists before a transfer is released. A potential match puts the transfer on hold. The transfer can only be released by the Compliance team after the match is reviewed and documented as a false positive.

### 2.2 Automatic holds

A transfer is held automatically when:

- the amount is 5,000 KWD or more in a single transaction;
- the customer has sent more than 5 transfers to different beneficiaries in 24 hours;
- the beneficiary is in a high-risk jurisdiction;
- the customer's profile is missing mandatory CDD information.

Held transfers must be reviewed within 4 working hours.

## 3. Daily batch review

The monitoring system runs every night at 01:00 and flags:

- structuring: several transactions just below the 3,000 KWD CDD threshold within 7 days;
- sudden changes in behaviour, such as a customer whose monthly volume is more than three times their 6-month average;
- many unrelated senders paying the same beneficiary.

Alerts from the batch run must be assigned to an analyst by 10:00 on the next working day.

## 4. Alert handling

The analyst reviews the customer history, CDD file and any notes from branch staff. Each alert is closed with one of three outcomes: false positive, monitor, or escalate. The reason must be written in the case notes; "no issue" alone is not an acceptable closing note.

Escalated cases go to the Compliance Officer, who decides within 2 working days whether to file a suspicious transaction report (STR) with the Financial Intelligence Unit.

## 5. Tipping off

Staff must never inform a customer that their transaction is under review for suspicion of money laundering. If a customer asks why a transfer is delayed, staff should say it is undergoing standard checks.
